Features

Everything you need for two screens, and nothing else.

Wallps is a small menu bar app. You set it up once and it keeps working through every lock, sleep, logout and restart.

Two screens, two wallpapers

Pick one image for your desktop and another for your lock screen. Each stays where you put it.

No flash when you lock

Wallps swaps before the lock screen draws, so you never catch your desktop wallpaper behind it.

Live lock screens

Use a video or one of Apple's 4K aerials as a moving lock screen on macOS 26 and later.

Apple's whole catalog

Browse every Apple wallpaper and aerial by macOS release or theme. Each one downloads the first time you use it.

Swap in one click

Flip your desktop and lock screen wallpapers from the window or straight from the menu bar.

Every display

Works across multiple monitors and picks back up after your MacBook wakes from lid sleep.

How it works

Set it up in under a minute.

Pick two wallpapers

Drag in an image or video, choose a file, or browse Apple's catalog for each screen.

Press Apply

Both wallpapers are set at once. Turn on Launch at Login so it keeps working after restarts.

Lock your Mac

Press ⌃ ⌘ Q. Your lock screen shows its own wallpaper, every time.

Questions

Good to know.

Why does macOS block Wallps the first time?

Wallps is free and isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer ID yet, so Gatekeeper quarantines it. The Terminal command above removes that flag for Wallps only. You can also read the source and build it yourself.

Do I need to keep Wallps running?

Yes. Wallps lives in the menu bar and does the switching. Closing the window keeps it running. Turn on Launch at Login and it starts quietly after every restart. If you quit it, your Mac goes back to using one wallpaper for both screens.

Which Macs does it support?

Any Mac on macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon and Intel. Live lock screens (videos and aerials) need macOS 26 Tahoe or later.

Can it change the FileVault unlock screen?

No. On a Mac with FileVault, the first unlock screen after a restart runs before macOS loads, and no app can change it. Every lock after that shows your Wallps lock screen.

Does Wallps collect any data?

No. Your wallpapers stay on your Mac, there's no account, and there are no analytics. The only network requests are downloads from Apple's servers when you pick a wallpaper from the catalog. See the privacy policy.
